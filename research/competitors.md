# Montana Drone Services — Competitor Research & Gap Analysis

*Prepared for a new Montana-based commercial drone services company (working name: [COMPANY NAME]). Research conducted November 2026 via public web search and site fetches. Every claim below is sourced; where a source was unreachable or a claim could not be verified, that is stated explicitly rather than guessed.*

## 1. Montana Drone Company (Bozeman / Kalispell / Helena)

**Sources:** [montanadronecompany.com](http://www.montanadronecompany.com), [Bozeman Chamber of Commerce listing](https://members.bozemanchamber.com/list/member/montana-drone-company-7698)

- **Services:** Video production and photography (ground and aerial), real estate media, business marketing video, mapping/surveying, construction documentation, product video, event coverage, interviews, a "360 aerial tour" product, drone inspections, a "Drone Experience" offering, Part 107 certification prep/flight training, and an online store selling prints, stock footage, and apparel.
- **Page structure/navigation:** Header has Home, Services, Projects, FAQ, About, a "More Info" dropdown (News, Contact, Locations, Photo Galleries, Store, Montana Drone Laws), and a "Book Now" button. Body sections run: hero, featured monthly photo, featured video projects, testimonials, services grid, per-city links, video category pages, property/land listings, and a newsletter signup. Footer duplicates most menu links.
- **Calls to action:** "Book Now" (repeated many times), "Contact Us," "Sample Projects," "Demo Reel," "Shop," "Sign Up Today."
- **Trust signals:** Claims to have been voted Montana's best aerial photographers "2024–2026" by 130,000 voters, with no linked source. States it has supplied footage to Netflix, Paramount, CBS, and Warner Brothers, named as text only, no logos or project detail. Four written testimonials from named individuals with no company affiliation or project context. Claims "over $1 billion" in completed real estate work and "hundreds" of Montana business clients. None of these claims link to a verifiable source. No insurance figures or named pilot certifications appear anywhere on the site.
- **Pricing visibility:** None. No rates, packages, or quote calculator — every path funnels to "Book Now" or a contact form.
- **Gov/enterprise positioning:** None. No government case studies, no capability statement, no mention of public-sector work.
- **SEO approach:** Dedicated landing pages for Bozeman, Billings, Butte, Great Falls, Helena, Kalispell, Miles City, and Missoula, following a consistent "[city]-mt-video-production-photographer" URL pattern, plus a "We Cover All Montana" hub linking all of them. This is a reasonably solid local-SEO structure.
- **Weaknesses a stronger competitor could beat:** Inconsistent branding (the business calls itself "Montana Drone Company," "MT Drone Co," and "MDC" in different places); repetitive, cluttered navigation (the same links appear in header, a "More Info" submenu, and footer); unverifiable superlative claims (the "130,000 voters" award, unnamed Netflix/Paramount work) that read as marketing noise rather than credibility; thin testimonials with no context; zero pricing transparency; zero government/enterprise content; and a blended focus (property listings sit next to production work), which muddies the value proposition for a buyer trying to assess professionalism.

## 2. Montex Aerial Ops LLC (Bozeman)

**Sources:** Droners.io marketplace listings (no independent company website or FAA record found)

- **Findings:** Montex Aerial Ops LLC does not appear to have its own website, FAA public record, or independent review presence that this research could locate. It exists only as a pilot profile on the Droners.io marketplace.
- **Services claimed:** One Droners.io listing describes the operator as Part 107–certified, specializing in landscape, real estate, and commercial work, based in Bozeman with a stated 150-mile travel radius and $1.0 million in listed insurance coverage, at an average rate of $150/hr. A separate listing under the same name, in the real-estate/mapping category, describes "2D/3D mapping, modeling & photogrammetry" with no rate shown.
- **Data quality problem:** The two listings show different FAA license numbers (#4613670 vs. #5324660), and the second number also appears attached to other, unrelated pilot profiles on the same marketplace — suggesting the directory data is unreliable or templated rather than individually verified.
- **Weakness for the gap analysis:** This is effectively a marketplace listing, not a company with a credible independent web presence — there is no site, no portfolio, no case studies, and no way to verify the claimed credentials without contacting the operator directly. A competitor with an actual, verifiable, professionally built website would look categorically more credible by comparison.

## 4. Advanced Aerial Applications LLC

**Sources:** Droners.io marketplace listing (no independent company website found)

## 5. Avion Unmanned (most important — government/public-agency positioning)

**Sources:** [Montana participating-addendum press release, SUAS News, Jan 2020](https://www.suasnews.com/2020/01/montana-signs-participating-addendum-with-avion-unmanned-to-provide-drone-services-to-public-agencies/); [Avion Unmanned multi-state contract win, SUAS News, 2019](https://www.suasnews.com/2019/08/avion-unmanned-wins-multi-state-contract-to-provide-unmanned-aircraft-system-services-for-the-public-sector/); [Versa Integrity Group acquisition announcement, SUAS News, Jan 2022](https://www.suasnews.com/2022/01/versa-integrity-group-acquires-avion-solutions-unmanned-aircraft-systems-commercial-operations/); current [avionsolutions.com](https://www.avionsolutions.com) homepage (fetched directly); attempted fetch of avionsolutions.com/unmanned (404); NASPO ValuePoint portfolio references found via secondary sources (direct naspovaluepoint.org portfolio page returned 404 on fetch).

**This is the single most important finding in this research, so it is reported in detail.**

### What Avion Unmanned's government positioning actually was
- In August 2019, Virginia's Department of General Services awarded a multi-state cooperative contract (Master Agreement E194-79435) for "Unmanned Aerial Vehicle / Drone Services" through the NASPO ValuePoint cooperative purchasing organization. Reported awardees on that Virginia-led vehicle included Avion Solutions (Avion Unmanned), DroneUp, Measure UAS, and Draper Aden Associates.
- States then signed individual "participating addenda" to piggyback on that master agreement without running their own RFP. Montana signed a participating addendum with Avion Unmanned in January 2020, covering "the State and its agencies, political subdivisions, and institutions." DroneUp separately signed its own Montana addendum around the same time — meaning Montana approved more than one vendor under this vehicle.
- Avion Unmanned's listed public-sector service categories were: emergency services, law enforcement support, aerial inspection and mapping data services, support to agriculture and gaming, and agency media relations/marketing.
- Avion Unmanned was the UAS division of Avion Solutions, Inc., a Huntsville, Alabama, employee-owned defense/aviation engineering firm serving DoD and commercial clients since 1992.

### What appears to be true today (and this matters a great deal for positioning)
- In January 2022, trade press (SUAS News, UAS Vision) reported that **Versa Integrity Group** (a Dorilton Capital–backed industrial inspection platform) **acquired Avion Solutions' commercial UAS operations** ("Avion Commercial"), with Avion Solutions retaining its defense/government engineering business.
- A direct fetch of Avion Solutions' current homepage (avionsolutions.com) in November 2026 shows **no mention of "Unmanned," UAS, or drones anywhere** in navigation or content. The site's listed capabilities are now Technology Development and Design, Airworthiness Certification, Program Support, Lifecycle Engineering, Integrated Product Support, Systems Engineering, Testing and Evaluation, and Modeling and Simulation — all tied to aviation/missile/directed-energy defense programs. A direct fetch of avionsolutions.com/unmanned returned **HTTP 404**.
- Separately, secondary sources indicate the underlying NASPO ValuePoint "Unmanned Aerial Vehicle/Drone Services" portfolio (2019–2024) **sunset on October 31, 2024, with NASPO explicitly deciding not to resolicit that specific portfolio.** One state procurement office (Alaska) was reported considering a state-level replacement contract instead. This could not be independently re-confirmed against the live NASPO portfolio page (naspovaluepoint.org/portfolio/unmanned-aerial-vehicle-drone-services-2019-2024 returned 404 on fetch), so treat the sunset date as reported-but-not-directly-verified, and confirm against NASPO's current portfolio listing before quoting it externally.
- Consistent with a lapsed vehicle, Montana's own more recent (spring 2026) drone-related state procurement activity found in this research was for **direct hardware purchases** — e.g., Montana DEQ solicitations for drone systems (including a FreeFly Astro Max unit awarded to Unmanned Vehicle Technologies for $28,995 in June 2026) — rather than any visible drone-as-a-service task order issued under the old NASPO/Avion vehicle.

### What this means
Avion Unmanned's 2020 Montana addendum is the most-cited "gold standard" government relationship in this space, but the trail strongly suggests that relationship is now dormant: the parent brand no longer markets a drone division, the commercial UAS line was divested to a different company in 2022, and the national contract vehicle underneath the addendum appears to have expired without renewal. **No competitor researched here currently has a visibly active, current Montana government drone-services contract.** That is a meaningful opening rather than a moat to beat.

### What public-agency buyers seem to respond to (based on the pattern across states)
- A pre-approved vendor status under a cooperative contract (so the agency doesn't need to run its own RFP) was the core value proposition across every state addendum found (Montana, Utah, New Mexico, Connecticut, North Dakota, South Dakota, Alaska, Iowa, Hawaii, Arizona, Rhode Island, Florida).
- Named, government-relevant service categories (emergency/disaster response, law enforcement support, infrastructure inspection, agriculture, media/PIO support) rather than generic "aerial photography" language.
- A defense/engineering-credible parent brand (Avion Solutions' DoD pedigree) was clearly leveraged as the underlying trust signal, more than any drone-specific marketing page.

Takeaway: clean, scalable template-driven structure; the national network model trades local authenticity for breadth and SEO reach into multiple Montana cities from one domain.

### Voltair (Flathead Electric Cooperative utility inspection contractor)
[Daily Interlake, Apr 29 2026](https://dailyinterlake.com/news/2026/apr/29/flathead-electric-cooperative-conducts-aerial-inspections-of-power-lines/); [Western News, Apr 24 2026](https://thewesternnews.com/news/2026/apr/24/fec-using-drones-for-power-line-inspections/) — Flathead Electric Cooperative (northwest Montana) contracted Voltair, described as a "certified utility inspection contractor," to inspect power lines and equipment across its service territory in 2026. Voltair is a Y Combinator (Winter 2026) startup based in San Francisco building hybrid fixed-wing drones that recharge directly on power lines, operating under Part 107 BVLOS waivers using "shielded area" exceptions near power infrastructure, anticipating the FAA's Part 108 BVLOS rule later in 2026–2027. Inspection crews and vehicles carry both FEC and Voltair identification; personnel carry photo ID. A reported $12M Series A (March 2026) came from a lower-quality source and could not be independently confirmed. **Relevance:** this is the clearest example in Montana right now of a utility choosing a highly technical, safety-and-compliance-forward outside contractor over a local generalist drone shop — it sets the credibility bar for utility/infrastructure work (named BVLOS authorization pathway, branded vehicles/ID badges, explicit safety communication to the public) that a Montana-based competitor should be able to match in presentation even before matching in technology.

### Skyfish (Stevensville, MT) — hardware maker, not a service provider
[skyfish.ai](https://www.skyfish.ai) — Montana-based (Stevensville) enterprise drone manufacturer, founded 2014, building the M4 and newer Osprey platforms for infrastructure inspection and survey-grade 3D modeling. Positions explicitly for "enterprise, government, and defense customers." Its primary trust signals are compliance badges — "Blue UAS Cleared, Green UAS Certified, NDAA compliant" plus an "American Made" badge — rather than named client logos or case studies (none were found on the fetched homepage). Tagline language is confident and nationalistic: "Trusted American Autonomy," "Full Stack. Mission Ready.," "Down to Every Cotter Pin." Navigation: Drones / Technology / Industries (cell tower, power, transportation, tactical ISR) / Resources / Contact, with CTAs of "Book A Call" and "Book A Demo." **Relevance:** Skyfish is not a direct services competitor, but its badge-and-compliance-language approach (naming specific certifications like Blue UAS/NDAA rather than vague "certified professionals" claims) is a strong, directly reusable pattern for a services company's own credibility section — and its Montana manufacturing location is a legitimate "we fly Montana-made, NDAA-compliant hardware" talking point a new local operator could consider.

### Marketplaces — Droners.io and Click Rally (pricing signals)

Structure: hero with "post a gig" / "become an operator" CTAs, service categories (real estate, construction/land, events/venues), four-step how-it-works, FAQ, other-Montana-cities links.

## Government/Enterprise positioning — focused analysis

Avion Unmanned's Montana NASPO ValuePoint participating addendum (signed January 2020) is the most substantial public-agency relationship found among the five primary competitors — but the evidence assembled above (Avion Solutions' current site makes zero mention of drones; the commercial UAS unit was sold to Versa Integrity Group in January 2022; the underlying NASPO drone-services portfolio reportedly sunset in October 2024 without resolicitation; Montana's 2026 drone procurement activity is hardware purchases, not service task orders) points to that relationship being **stale or defunct in practice, even though the press-release trail makes it look current if you don't dig past 2020.**

What this means for a new entrant:
1. **The "incumbent" government relationship is largely a ghost.** No competitor in this set has a demonstrably live Montana government drone-services contract today. This is an open lane, not a wall to climb.
2. **What actually earns public-agency trust, based on the pattern across every state addendum found:** being pre-vetted on a cooperative/state contract vehicle so agencies skip their own RFP; clearly named, government-relevant service categories (emergency response, law enforcement support, infrastructure inspection/mapping, agriculture) instead of generic "aerial photography" language; and an institutionally credible parent brand or formal capability statement rather than a marketing-driven homepage.
3. **Montana itself is still actively buying drone capability** (DEQ's 2026 hardware solicitations/awards), which confirms real, current agency demand exists — it's just currently being met by equipment purchases rather than a services contract, which is itself an opening for a services-and-training pitch ("we can fly it and maintain the program so your staff doesn't have to become FAA-certified operators").

## Gap Analysis — what would make a brand-new Montana drone company look more professional and government-ready

**Ground rule used throughout:** the company has zero pilots, aircraft, clients, or completed projects today. Every recommendation below leans on honest, flag-driven transparency about that — not fabricated badges, invented testimonials, or vague "certified professionals" language like the pattern seen at Montana Drone Company. Every competitor profiled above is beatable on *structure and credibility design*, not on fake track record.

### 1. Structure: separate commercial and government/enterprise tracks
None of the five primary competitors has a dedicated, well-developed government/public-agency section — Avion Unmanned's positioning exists only in 2019–2020 press releases, not on a live page. A distinct "For Government & Public Agencies" top-level page (separate from a "For Business" commercial page) immediately reads as more enterprise-ready than every local competitor, none of whom segment their audience at all. This page should contain: a one-paragraph capability statement, NAICS codes (commonly used in this space: 541370 Surveying and Mapping Services, 541360 Geophysical Surveying and Mapping Services, 541922 Commercial Photography — confirm exact fit before publishing), a downloadable one-page capability statement PDF in the format federal/state buyers expect, and placeholders clearly marked "in progress" for UEI/SAM.gov registration, insurance binder, and FAA certifications rather than fabricated numbers.

### 2. Trust-signal design: honesty as the differentiator, not fake badges
Every local competitor either shows no trust signals (Montex, Iron Eagle — no site at all) or shows unverifiable ones (Montana Drone Company's "130,000 voters" award, unnamed Netflix/Paramount claims). A new company can out-credibility all of them by doing the opposite: a visible, dated "Where We Are Today" section stating plainly that the company is newly formed, pilots/aircraft are being onboarded, insurance is bound before first flight, and listing the specific certifications being pursued (Part 107, Part 107 waivers/BVLOS roadmap, Remote ID compliance) with real target dates. This "flag-driven honesty" approach — status flags instead of static badges — is more convincing to a sophisticated government or enterprise buyer than any unverified superlative, and it's a structural pattern none of these competitors use.

### 3. Content depth: capability statement, NAICS, SAM.gov readiness
Nothing in this entire competitive set (including Avion Unmanned's current public footprint) presents a modern, federal-style capability statement. Building one now — core competencies, differentiators, NAICS codes, bonding/insurance capacity, safety management approach, and a "past performance" section explicitly marked as forthcoming — puts the new company's documentation ahead of every local competitor immediately, independent of actual flight hours logged.

### 4. Certification and compliance language, borrowed in spirit (not copied) from Skyfish and Voltair
Skyfish's pattern of naming specific, checkable compliance items (Blue UAS, NDAA compliance, "American Made") rather than vague claims is worth emulating structurally: name the actual FAA Part 107 framework, Remote ID compliance, and (as Part 108 BVLOS rules mature, per the Voltair example) a stated roadmap toward BVLOS authorization. Pair this with an explicit data-handling/chain-of-custody statement for government and utility imagery — something **no competitor in this research mentions at all** — which matters disproportionately to public-agency and utility buyers handling sensitive infrastructure data.

### 5. SEO/location-page structure worth emulating (structure only, not content)
Montana Drone Company's and Dronegenuity's per-city landing pages (Bozeman, Billings, Missoula, Kalispell, Helena, Great Falls, Butte) are a legitimate, working local-SEO pattern. A stronger version would organize pages by **region + service vertical** rather than city + generic "video production," e.g., a northwest Montana utility-inspection page (echoing the real Flathead Electric/Voltair demand), a Golden Triangle agricultural-mapping page, a Bozeman–Missoula growth-corridor construction-progress page, and a statewide government/emergency-services page — mirroring real, evidenced demand (DEQ equipment purchases, FEC's utility contract, agricultural mapping interest) rather than copying any competitor's photo-gallery-driven city pages.

### 6. Pricing transparency as a differentiator
Every competitor with a real website (Montana Drone Company, Dronegenuity, Click Rally) hides pricing behind a quote form. Government and enterprise RFPs typically expect rate transparency up front. Publishing an illustrative "starting at" rate card by service tier — even caveated as subject to scope — would be a structural first-mover move in this specific competitive set and signals professionalism to exactly the institutional buyers this company wants to attract.

### 7. Single consistent brand identity
Montana Drone Company's inconsistent self-naming ("Montana Drone Company" / "MT Drone Co" / "MDC") and cluttered, repeated navigation is an easy, low-cost professionalism gap to avoid outright from day one: one name, one clean nav, no duplicate menu structures.

**Bottom line of the gap analysis:** this is a weak field. Two of the five named competitors (Montex, Iron Eagle) have no real website at all — only unverified marketplace listings with inconsistent data. The one with the strongest public credentials (Advanced Aerial Applications) has no site to showcase them. The one positioned as the government gold standard (Avion Unmanned) appears to have largely exited the business, and its underlying national contract vehicle has reportedly lapsed. A new company that simply builds a clean, honestly-labeled, government-structured website with a real capability statement and transparent compliance roadmap would already be the most professional-looking player in this set — before flying a single mission.
